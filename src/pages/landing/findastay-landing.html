The manifesto

A New Era in
Accommodation Pricing

For twenty years the price of a room has been something done to you. That era is ending.

The problem

Everyone negotiates. You just weren't invited.

A hotel room is not a tin of beans. Its value changes by the hour — a rainy Tuesday in winter is worth a fraction of a Saturday in summer, and owners know it.

So they discount constantly: to corporate contracts, to the big booking sites, to wholesalers, to the walk-in who asks at 8pm. The one person never offered the real price is the traveller booking online weeks ahead. You see one fixed number — and sitting inside it is a 15–18% commission the property pays the booking site and quietly builds back into your rate.

The discount already exists. We just hand you the keys to it.
The idea

Flip the transaction.

Instead of a property shouting one price into the void, the traveller opens the conversation: here's what I'll pay for these nights. The property looks at its real occupancy and answers in seconds — yes, here's my counter, or not this time.

It's how markets have always worked everywhere except the one screen you book a holiday on. No auctions, no gamesmanship, no waiting. One offer, one answer.

And that "middleman"? It's not local.

Booking.com and Expedia are offshore giants. Their commission doesn't just disappear from the price — it leaves the country entirely. Every dollar that goes to them is a dollar that never reaches the family that hosted you, never lands in the town you stayed in, and never circulates back home.

findastay keeps the whole thing between two people: you and the local who hosts you. The money you save stays in your pocket. The money they earn stays in their community. Nothing gets siphoned overseas. That's the real point — not just a cheaper night, but a fairer one.

Why now

The tech finally caught up.

Handling thousands of individual offers used to be impossible — no front desk could keep up. AI changes that. An assistant prices a fair offer, drafts it politely, and lets an owner clear a night's offers in the time it takes to make a coffee.

That's the unlock: negotiation at the scale of the whole internet, without the friction that made it unworkable before.

Fixed pricing was a limit of the old tools. It was never the fair outcome.
Questions, answered

Everything you're wondering.

New way to book, so new questions. Straight talk for travellers and owners.

How is findastay different from Booking.com or Airbnb?
There you accept a fixed price with commission built in. Here you make the offer — your price, your dates — and the property accepts, counters once, or declines. You deal with the owner directly.
Who pays for what?
You pay the property for your stay, and you pay findastay one small commitment fee when you confirm a booking. That's it — no booking fees, no markup on the rate.
What is the commitment fee, and how much?
It's a small fee that locks in a confirmed booking. It depends on your booking total: $9.99 under $500, $14.99 up to $1,000, $24.99 over $1,000. It comes out of the booking total — the property receives the rest.

Example: you offer $150/night for 2 nights = $300. On confirming you pay findastay the $9.99 fee and the property $290.01 for your stay — $300 in total, nothing extra.
Is making an offer free?
Yes. Browsing and making offers cost nothing. You only pay the commitment fee if an offer is accepted and you choose to confirm.
Is the commitment fee refundable?
It's non-refundable once the property accepts and you confirm — that's what secures the booking for you. If the property says no, you pay nothing.
How do I pay for the stay itself?
The property collects the stay balance directly — by bank transfer, on arrival, or their own method. findastay only ever takes the commitment fee; we never hold your stay payment.
How do I know a fair price to offer?
The AI concierge suggests a sensible figure from your dates, the property and typical rates — so you're not lowballing or overpaying. Adjust it before it sends.
When do I get the property's details, and can I message them?
The moment your booking is confirmed, a private chat opens with the property and your contact details are shared so they can book you in. The thread stays open until 10 days after checkout.
What does my business pay?
No commission, no subscription, no listing fee. The traveller pays a small commitment fee that findastay retains, and it comes off the final payment that reaches you — that's the only deduction, and you never get a bill from us.

Example: a booking totals $300. findastay keeps the $9.99 fee and you receive $290.01. On a $600 booking the fee is $14.99 (you net $585.01); over $1,000 it's $24.99 (you net the total − $24.99).
Who pays for what?
The traveller pays you for the stay (directly, by your own method) and pays findastay the commitment fee. Two separate things — and none of it is a commission on your rate.
How much is the fee, and who pays it?
The traveller pays it, based on the booking total: $9.99 under $500, $14.99 up to $1,000, $24.99 over $1,000. It's taken from the booking total, so you net the rest.
How do I get paid?
You collect the stay balance directly from the guest, your way: bank transfer/deposit, on arrival, card on your own terminal, or your own payment link. findastay never holds your money.
Can I choose which payment methods I accept?
Yes. Tick the methods you take when you set up — and change them any time from your dashboard.
Do I have to accept low offers?
Never. You set your floor. Every offer hits your dashboard and you accept, counter once, or decline. You're always in control of rates and availability.
Won't this undercut my normal rates?
It fills nights you'd otherwise leave empty. A room sold at a fair offer beats an empty room — and with no commission you often net more than a full-price booking-site sale.
When do I get the guest's contact details?
The moment a booking confirms you get the guest's name, email, phone and preferred contact method — plus a private chat thread — so you can book them straight in.
How long does setup take?
Minutes. Register, search and claim your property (or create it), check your rooms & photos, set your prices, and go live.
Can I still use other channels?
Yes. findastay is one more channel for filling rooms — run it alongside whatever you use now. Keep an eye on your availability so you don't double-book while we build calendar sync.
